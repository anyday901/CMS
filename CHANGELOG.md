# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Reports** in the admin menu, for staff with the new **See reports** permission (Admin and Billing roles have it). Income: annual income by month, monthly transactions, daily performance and an income forecast from active services. Sales: income by product, new orders and new clients by month, promotion usage and top clients by income. Invoices: aging by days overdue, credits given and used, and a client statement with a running balance. Tax: tax collected by country and rate, on invoices paid or issued, optionally EU clients only. Clients and services: clients by country and by group, growth and churn, retention time, suspensions and cancellations by reason, and server revenue. Support: tickets opened and closed, first response and resolution times, replies by staff, ratings and feedback comments, and tags. Each has a date range with shortcuts, a currency choice when you bill in several, and **Export to CSV**.
- The WHMCS import brings over the history reports read, so they cover the years before the switch: promotions, orders (keeping their numbers, linked to their services, invoice and promotion), the credit log (with a line making up any difference from the WHMCS balance), cancellation requests (carried out, or still waiting for services WHMCS hadn't cancelled) and the activity log. Imported tickets keep when an admin first replied, and cancelled services when they were cancelled. WHMCS admins whose role can manage invoices get reports too.
- The WHMCS import walkthrough explains that custom WHMCS reports (PHP files in `modules/reports`) can't be copied, since only the database is read, and lists the closest built-in report for each of WHMCS's own.
- **Settings > Automation**: the billing run's time and every number of days it works by (renewal invoices, paying from credit, overdue reminders, late fees, suspension, termination and overage invoices) in one place.
- **Settings > Cron status**: whether the scheduler is running, with the crontab line when it isn't; each automatic task's schedule, last run, result and next run; the queue's waiting and failed jobs; and recent runs, kept 30 days.
- One login for several client accounts, like WHMCS 8 users: give the same person portal access as a contact on each account, with the same email. Each account keeps its own contact for them (permissions, email copies), and the password, two-factor authentication and remember-me are shared, so a new password or two-factor change reaches every account. After logging in they choose an account, switch with the account name in the portal menu, and land on the one they used last. Someone who already signs in gets the new **Contact added to another account** email instead of an invitation. The WHMCS import gives a WHMCS 8 user of several clients one login for all of them.
- Remember-me for contacts, with its own cookie that signs them back in as the contact (never as the account holder) and ends when they log out or change their password.
- Emails about logins from new devices: clients and contacts get the new **New device sign-in** email, with the time, browser and IP address, when their email signs in to the portal from a browser it hasn't used before. The first browser is just noted. On by default; turn it off under **Settings > Security** (or `SECURITY_NEW_DEVICE_EMAILS`). Browsers unused for a year are forgotten, apart from each email's most recent one, so a login after a long break is still emailed about.
- Client notes: dated notes by staff on a client's page, with who wrote them, and pinned notes shown as a banner at the top. The old notes field is now **Profile notes**.
- A to-do list for staff under **To-do**: items for one staff member or anyone, with details, a due date, a status and optionally a client. Tabs for mine, everyone's, unassigned, overdue and done; a tick box marks one done; the dashboard lists yours and the menu counts them. A client's page lists and adds its to-dos.
- Client groups under **Settings > Client groups**, with a colour, shown as a label on clients and filterable in the clients search (including clients in no group).
- A search box in the admin menu that looks through clients (and their contacts), services and domains, invoices, orders and tickets at once. Staff who can't work on tickets don't see them.
- Merge two client accounts: **Merge** on a client's page moves everything (services, invoices, payments, orders, tickets, contacts, notes, to-dos, emails, logins and activity) to another account, adds its credit balance there, keeps its details in a note and deletes it. Contacts with the same email are combined, and the merged account holder can be kept as a contact. Accounts in different currencies can't be merged.
- Marketing email consent: a box at sign-up (unticked by default), **Email preferences** under **Account** in the portal, and a box on the client's edit form for staff. Each change is logged with when it was made. Each client has a signed unsubscribe link that works without logging in and supports one-click unsubscribe from mail apps. The clients search filters by it, and the data export includes it.
- **Export to CSV** on the clients list downloads the clients found, with their group, marketing consent and unsubscribe link. Values a spreadsheet would run as a formula are written as text.
- The WHMCS import brings over client groups (noting discounts and exemptions they had, which groups here don't do), staff notes on clients with their pinned flag, the to-do list, and marketing email opt-in.
- Two-factor authentication with an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, 1Password and others) for clients, contacts and staff. Clients and contacts turn it on under the portal's new **Security** page, staff under **My account** (their name in the admin menu): scan a QR code, type the first code, and keep eight one-time recovery codes. Logging in then asks for a code after the password; a code can't be used twice, and five wrong codes start the login over. Turning it off or making new recovery codes takes the password. Staff can turn it off for a client, a contact or (admins) another staff member who lost their phone. **Settings > Security** can require it for all staff. Secrets and recovery codes are stored encrypted.
- Contacts can have their own portal login. Tick **Can sign in to the portal** on a contact (staff on the client's page, clients under **Account**) and choose what they can do: invoices (see and pay, use credit), orders (place orders, upgrade and cancel services), services (see services, control panel logins, service passwords) and support (tickets). The contact is emailed the new **Contact portal invitation** with a link to choose a password (it lasts a day; **Send invite again** or **Forgot password** gets a new one). Signed in, a contact sees only those parts of the account, never its details or other contacts; tickets they write and what they do in the activity log show their own name. Taking access away signs them out at once. A login email has to be unique among clients and contacts who sign in. Contacts have no remember-me, since it would sign in the account holder. The WHMCS import brings sub-account contacts and WHMCS 8 users of a client over with portal access and matching permissions; they set a new password with **Forgot password**.
- Email address verification: clients who sign up at checkout, or change their email in the portal, get the new **Email verification** email with a link to confirm the address. Until then the portal shows a reminder with **Send the link again**. Staff see whether an address is verified on the client's page and can send the link or mark it verified; changing it in the admin area makes it unverified again. Clients from before this change, and clients imported from WHMCS, count as verified.
- Login history: every portal and admin login, successful or not, with the IP address and browser. Clients see recent logins to their account (contacts' too) under **Security**, staff see their own under **My account**, a client's page shows their last five, and **Settings > Security** has the full history with search and filters. It keeps 90 days by default.
- Failed login lockout: after 5 failed logins (password or two-factor code) from one IP address to one account in 15 minutes, that address is locked out of the account until enough of them are older than 15 minutes. The real owner can still log in from elsewhere. Both numbers are on **Settings > Security**.
- IP bans: an address that fails 20 logins in an hour, to any accounts, is banned for 24 hours (both changeable, or off). Staff ban an address or range such as `203.0.113.0/24`, for some days or for good, and lift bans, under **Settings > Security**. Banned addresses get a 403 on every page except payment and email webhooks, and you can't ban your own address. Ended bans are removed every night.
- CAPTCHA on public forms with Cloudflare Turnstile, hCaptcha or Google reCAPTCHA v2: pick the provider, paste its site and secret keys (the secret is stored encrypted) and choose the forms under **Settings > Security**: portal login, sign-up at checkout, forgot password, the contact form, and the staff login. If the provider can't be reached, the form goes through rather than locking everyone out.
- GDPR tools. **Export personal data** on a client's page, and **Download my data** in the portal, give a JSON file of everything kept about the account: details, contacts, services, orders, invoices with payments, credit, tickets (without staff's internal notes), emails, logins and activity, without passwords or security codes. **Anonymise this client**, on a closed account with no running services, removes their name, email, phone, address, notes and custom fields, deletes their contacts, emails and login history, blanks what was said in their tickets and their attachments, and takes their name and IP addresses out of the activity log, while keeping invoices, payments and services for the books.
- Staff change their own password under **My account**.
- Translations of knowledge base category names and descriptions, under **Translations** on a category's edit page. Visitors see them on the knowledge base home page, category pages, article breadcrumbs and search results; a translation without a description shows the main one. The WHMCS import brings over category translations too.
- The WHMCS import gives each admin the role their WHMCS role's permissions add up to: a built-in role when one matches, otherwise the custom role with just the abilities those permissions grant (such as clients and tickets but not billing). Roles with every permission a Full Administrator has become Admin. The import report says what each admin got and from which WHMCS permissions.
- Tickets from people without a client account (guest tickets), off by default: turn on **Accept tickets from people without an account** under **Settings** (or `TICKETS_GUEST_TICKETS`). Then an email to a department from an unknown address opens a ticket, and visitors can use the new contact form at `/support/contact` (linked from the help pages). The guest is emailed a private link to the ticket, where they read and answer replies, close it and rate the support; the form never shows the link itself, so only whoever reads that inbox can follow the ticket. Replies by email from the same address thread onto it. Staff see the guest's name and address in ticket lists and can move a guest's ticket to a client account, which turns the link off. Only tickets from the same guest address can be merged, and splitting a guest ticket always emails the guest a link to the new one.
- A spam filter for piped ticket email and the contact form, under **Settings > Ticket spam filter**: drop email the mail server's spam checker (SpamAssassin, Rspamd) marked as spam, blocked senders and whole domains, blocked words in the subject or message, and a limit on new tickets one address can open in an hour (10 by default; for a client, only emails from that address count, not the account's portal tickets). Bounces and email from the business's own addresses are ignored, so they can't loop. The contact form also has a hidden field that catches bots, and answers spam as if it were sent.
- **Tickets > Email and spam log** lists every email piped in for tickets, and contact form messages caught as spam, with what became of each (opened a ticket, replied, ignored or spam) and why. It keeps 90 days.
- Translations of knowledge base articles and announcements. Set the main language and the other languages under **Settings > Languages**, then translate each article or announcement on its edit page. Visitors see the translation into the language their browser asks for, or pick one on the help pages; search and ticket suggestions match the translation too, and anything not translated shows in the main language.
- The WHMCS import brings over admins as staff accounts (matched by email to existing staff, Full Administrators as admins and other roles by what their permissions add up to, disabled admins left out, and bcrypt passwords working as they were), so imported escalation rules keep everyone they assigned or emailed. Imported tickets keep who they were assigned to, and staff replies are linked to the staff member who wrote them, so feedback averages per staff member include them. It also brings over ticket feedback ratings (WHMCS's ratings out of 10 become out of 5, one per ticket), tickets from people without a client account as guest tickets, and the translations of knowledge base articles and announcements, offering their languages and taking WHMCS's default language as the main one.
- The WHMCS import brings over the knowledge base (categories, articles converted from HTML to Markdown, tags, views and helpful votes), announcements, network issues, downloads with their files (from WHMCS's downloads folder, a new optional field in the walkthrough and `--downloads=` on `php artisan whmcs:import`) and their products, ticket custom fields with their values, ticket tags, and escalation rules. Escalation rules come over turned off, and knowledge base articles that were for logged-in clients only come over unpublished, so both can be checked first. Tickets merged in WHMCS come over pointing at the ticket they were merged into instead of being skipped.
- Business hours for response time targets: opening hours for each day of the week, closed dates such as public holidays, and a timezone under **Settings > Business hours**. A department set to **Count business hours only** measures its targets in those hours, so a ticket that comes in at night is due once you open. Changing the hours updates waiting tickets.
- As a client types a new ticket's subject and message in the portal, knowledge base articles that might answer it are suggested, to open in a new tab.
- Inactive answered tickets close on their own after the hours set under **Settings** (off by default, or `TICKETS_CLOSE_INACTIVE_HOURS`). `php artisan tickets:close-inactive` runs every 15 minutes from the scheduler and emails the client with the new **Ticket closed for inactivity** template; a reply opens the ticket again.
- Merge and split tickets. On a ticket, staff tick other tickets of the same client to merge into it: their messages move over, they close, and their numbers (links, the portal and email replies) lead to the merged ticket. Staff can also tick messages and split them into a new ticket for the same client, with its own subject, department and priority, optionally emailing the client the new number.
- Ticket tags: staff tag tickets on the ticket page, see tags in ticket lists and filter **Tickets** by tag. Tags no ticket uses any more are removed.
- Ticket feedback: a department can ask clients to rate the support from 1 to 5 stars, with a comment, when a ticket is closed. Staff closing a ticket, or it closing for inactivity, emails the new **Ticket feedback request**; clients rate on the ticket's portal page. Ratings show on the ticket and under **Tickets > Feedback**, with the average, how the ratings spread, and an average for each staff member who replied.
- Knowledge base at `/knowledgebase`: staff write Markdown articles under **Support > Knowledge base**, in categories one level deep, with tags, an order, and a published switch; categories can be hidden. Anyone can browse categories, search titles, text and tags, and read articles, which count their views, ask "Was this article helpful?" and list others in the same category. The portal's new ticket page points clients to it.
- Announcements at `/announcements`, with an RSS feed at `/announcements/rss`. Staff write them under **Support > Announcements** in Markdown with a date, so one can be written ahead, and a published switch. The latest three show on the portal home page.
- Network status page at `/network-status` with current issues, planned maintenance and issues resolved in the last 30 days. Staff add issues under **Support > Network status**, each affecting a server or something described in words, with a status (reported, investigating, in progress, outage, scheduled, resolved), priority, start and end, and resolve them in one click. Clients with an active service on an affected server see a notice on their portal home page. Servers with the new **Show on the network status page** setting are listed there with their monitoring status and 30-day uptime, without their host name or address.
- Downloads area at `/downloads`: files in categories, uploaded under **Support > Downloads** (up to 7.5 MB) and kept on the private disk. Each can be for everyone, for logged-in clients only, or for clients with an active service of chosen products; others see what they'd need. Downloads are counted.
- The portal's **Support** menu item covers tickets, the knowledge base, announcements, downloads and network status, with tabs between them, and the store menu has **Help** for visitors who aren't logged in.
- Ticket custom fields: under **Settings > Custom fields**, fields for tickets in every department or one department. Clients choose the department first and then fill in its fields (required ones must be filled); staff see and edit them all on the ticket, including staff-only fields, and may leave required ones empty.
- Response time targets (SLAs) per department, in hours by priority. A ticket waiting on staff is due that long after the client's last message. The tickets list shows when each is due, with an **Overdue** filter, and the ticket page shows when it was due and first answered. Changing a department's targets updates its waiting tickets.
- Ticket escalation rules under **Tickets > Escalation rules**: match open tickets by department, status, priority, time since the last reply and an overdue response, then move them to another department, change their status or priority, assign them, email the assignee or chosen staff, or post a reply to the client in the business's name. `php artisan tickets:escalate` runs them every five minutes from the scheduler; each rule changes a ticket once until the next reply.
- WHMCS import walkthrough under **Settings > Import from WHMCS**: a five-step guide with a progress bar that checks the install is empty, connects to the WHMCS database (the `WHMCS_DB_*` settings, or details typed in and kept encrypted in the staff member's session), runs a dry run and shows its record counts, totals compared and notes, then imports and lists what to do next. Both reports can be downloaded. It runs the same importer as `php artisan whmcs:import`.
- Public store at `/store`: active products listed by product group, each with an order page for its billing cycle, configurable options, add-ons, hostname and service custom fields. Staff set up groups (name, web address, description, order, shown or hidden) under **Products > Groups** and choose each product's group and sort order on the product form.
- Shopping cart at `/cart` and checkout at `/checkout`. Guests create their account at checkout (name, address, email, password and client custom fields), or log in and come back to it. Each checkout is saved as an order, creates its services as pending on one invoice, and sends the client to that invoice. An order with nothing to pay is marked paid and activated at once. Logged-in clients reach the store from **Order** in the portal, and the portal login page links to it.
- Product add-ons: a product marked as an add-on is offered with the products it is ticked on, at checkout or later from a service's page in the portal. Each add-on is its own service linked to the main one, billed on the same cycle where it's priced for it (otherwise once), and is suspended, unsuspended and terminated with its service.
- Promotion codes under **Products > Promotions**: percent or fixed amount off the products a code covers, for the first payment or every renewal, with optional start and expiry dates, maximum uses and a once-per-client limit. Codes are entered in the cart and checked again at checkout, where each use is counted. Discounts are shown as their own invoice lines, and a recurring discount is taken off each renewal.
- Upgrades and downgrades: staff choose which products a product's services can move to, and clients move from the service page in the portal, picking the new product's options. The rest of the paid period is prorated by day; a dearer product is invoiced and applied once paid, a cheaper one is applied at once with the difference added to the client's credit. Recurring promotion codes carry over when they cover the new product. The prorated period starts where the paid invoice line started it, so month-end services are prorated correctly; credits for cheaper moves include the tax paid on the unused time; and cancelling a move's unpaid invoice cancels the move.
- Billing on a fixed day of the month: a product's **Bill on day of the month** (1 to 28) makes every new service of it renew on that day. The first invoice is prorated from the order date to that day, and with **Also charge the next period from day** set, later orders pay the next whole cycle too. Add-ons without their own billing day follow the product they're ordered with, and upgrades in a prorated first period are worked out over the whole cycle.
- Free trials: a product's **Free trial days** make a client's first service of it free for that many days. The service starts at once with the trial on its order invoice at no charge (setup fees are still due), the portal and admin service pages show when the trial ends, and the first renewal invoice comes then. Each client gets one trial per product, on recurring billing cycles only, and upgrades wait until the trial ends.
- Stock control: products can count stock. Each store order takes one per service, the store shows the product as out of stock at none, and the cart and checkout refuse more than is left, with the last items locked so two checkouts can't both take them.
- Pending orders queue under **Orders** in the admin area, also listed on the dashboard and counted in the menu. Orders can be filtered by status and opened to see their client, IP address, invoice, promotion code and services. Billing staff accept, cancel or mark an order as fraud; cancelling or flagging cancels its unpaid invoice and services that haven't started, terminates running ones, and gives back its stock and promotion code use. **Hold new orders for review** on the **Settings** page (`BILLING_HOLD_NEW_ORDERS`) keeps a paid order's services pending until it's accepted.
- Client cancellation requests: clients ask to cancel a service from its portal page, straight away or at the end of the billing period, with an optional reason; staff can do the same for them from the admin service page. A request stops renewals and takes the service's unpaid charges off open invoices with nothing paid (cancelling an invoice with nothing else on it). End-of-period requests are carried out by `billing:run` on the due date and can be withdrawn until then. Cancelled services and their add-ons are marked cancelled and the module's terminate action runs. The dashboard lists upcoming cancellations.
- Staff upgrades and downgrades from the admin service page, to any product and recurring billing cycle, with or without charging. A charged move on the same cycle is prorated like a client's; one that changes the cycle credits the unused time and invoices a whole new period from today. A move without charging applies at once and bills the new price from the next renewal. A change of billing cycle alone doesn't run the provisioning module.
- Order confirmation email, sent when a client checks out, listing what they ordered with the order total and invoice link, and a separate **New order invoice** template for the first invoice of a store order (with the PDF attached when that's on). Both can be edited or turned off under **Email templates**.
- The admin orders list finds orders by order number or the client's name, company or email, and shows one client's orders; a client's page lists their latest orders.
- `App\Provisioning\ChangesPackage`: modules that implement it get a `change_package` action after an upgrade or downgrade. The manual fulfillment module opens a task with its own checklist, and the webhook module sends `change_package`.
- Support tickets: clients open tickets in the portal under **Support**, choosing a department and priority, and reply or close them there. Staff work them on a new **Tickets** page, filtered by status, department and "assigned to me", with the most urgent and longest waiting first. Staff reply, add internal notes clients never see, and change the status, priority, department and assignee. Clients and staff can attach up to 5 files of 10 MB each (images, PDFs, text, office documents and zip). Files are kept on the private disk and always downloaded, never shown in the browser.
- Ticket departments under **Settings > Departments**, each with an optional email address used as the reply-to on ticket emails and for piped mail.
- Inbound email for tickets: `php artisan tickets:pipe` reads an email on standard input (for a mail server pipe), and `POST /webhooks/email` takes the same raw email over HTTP when `TICKETS_INBOUND_SECRET` is set. A reply with `[Ticket #N]` in the subject from that ticket's client is added to the ticket; an email from a known client to a department address opens a new one. Quoted text and unsafe attachments are dropped, and strangers, auto-replies and bulk mail are ignored.
- Staff who handle tickets are emailed about new tickets and client replies; when a ticket is assigned, only the assignee is.
- Client emails: new invoice, overdue reminder, payment receipt, password link, ticket received and ticket reply emails, each with a default subject and Markdown body that staff can change or turn off under **Email templates**, using placeholders like `{invoice_number}`.
- Email log: every email to a client is listed under **Emails** with its status and full text, and a client's page shows their recent emails. Emails are sent from the queue and retried; staff can send a failed email again. Password links are left out of the log.
- New invoice emails attach the invoice PDF (turn off on the **Settings** page or with `BILLING_ATTACH_INVOICE_PDF=false`).
- Overdue reminders: `billing:run` emails a reminder when an unpaid invoice is overdue by each number of days in `BILLING_REMINDER_DAYS` (default 1 and 7).
- Custom staff role: tick exactly which abilities a staff member has (staff, settings, clients, billing, tickets) instead of using a built-in role.
- Activity log retention: set how many days to keep entries on the **Settings** page or with `ACTIVITY_RETENTION_DAYS`, and `php artisan activity:prune` (run nightly by the scheduler) deletes older ones.
- The dashboard lists tickets waiting on staff.
- Canned replies: staff save common ticket answers under **Tickets > Canned replies** and insert one into a reply from the ticket page. Placeholders such as `{client_first_name}`, `{ticket_id}` and `{staff_name}` are filled in for that ticket.
- Service welcome email, sent when a service is activated (its first invoice is paid, or staff add it without an invoice). Each product can add its own text to it, such as how to log in, with the new **Welcome email text** field. Staff can edit or turn it off like the other templates.
- Log in as client: staff who manage clients can open the portal as a client from the client's page. A banner shows while they do, everything they do there is logged under their own name, and **Back to admin** (or logging out of the portal) returns them to the client's page still signed in as staff.
- Client contacts: extra people on a client's account, added by staff on the client's page or by the client under **Account**. Contacts can be copied on invoice emails, support emails or both, and a contact who gets support emails can open and reply to tickets by email. The email log shows who was copied.
- Configurable options on products: dropdowns, checkboxes and quantities, each choice with a price per billing cycle and a setup fee. Options are picked when a service is added, priced into its recurring amount and first invoice, listed on its invoices, and can be changed later by staff.
- Custom fields on clients and services (text, text area, dropdown or checkbox, optionally required or staff only), set under **Settings > Custom fields**. Service fields can apply to every product or one product. Clients fill in their own fields on the portal account page and see service fields on the service page.
- The webhook provisioning module now sends custom fields by name and the service's configurable options.
- WHMCS importer: `php artisan whmcs:import` copies clients, contacts, products and prices, configurable options, custom fields, services, invoices, payments and refunds, credit balances, ticket departments, tickets, replies and (with `--attachments`) ticket attachments from a WHMCS database. Clients, services, invoices and tickets keep their WHMCS ids. `--dry-run` undoes everything afterwards, and every run compares totals with WHMCS and writes a report of skipped records to `storage/app/whmcs/`. Connection settings are `WHMCS_DB_*` in `.env`.
- Clients imported from WHMCS log in with their old password, which is then stored again with this app's hashing. Only bcrypt and Argon2 hashes are kept; clients with older md5-era hashes reset their password by email.
- `php artisan whmcs:compare` lists the renewals WHMCS and this app billed differently (amount or tax) for a period after the import, including WHMCS add-on and domain renewals, which aren't imported; `--simulate` generates this app's renewals for the period without saving them, so the two can run side by side before switching.
- Replies to old WHMCS ticket emails (`[Ticket ID: 123456]` in the subject) are added to the imported ticket.
- Triennial billing cycle.
- Install guide for Ubuntu with Nginx, PHP-FPM, MariaDB, cron and a systemd queue worker, with updates, backups and the move from WHMCS: [docs/install.md](docs/install.md).
- Docker Compose setup: `Dockerfile` and `docker-compose.yml` run the app with Nginx, MariaDB, Redis, a queue worker and the scheduler. The app image runs as www-data, the web image is unprivileged Nginx, and PHP session cookies are HttpOnly and Secure.
- Release checklist and steps for v1.0.0: [docs/release.md](docs/release.md).
- Servers and server groups under **Products > Servers**: each server has its module, host name, port, login (password and API token kept encrypted with the app key), maximum accounts and an on/off switch, with **Test connection** to check its details. Groups collect servers and fill them **least used** (each new account goes on the server with the most room) or **fill up** (one server is filled before the next). A product on a server module can pick a group; without one, the least used server of the module is chosen. A new service is put on a server when its create action runs, and the servers page shows how full each one is. A server can't be deleted while services are on it, or while an ended service's terminate is still waiting or failed. Host names can be IPv6 addresses.
- cPanel / WHM module: creates, suspends, unsuspends and removes cPanel accounts through WHM API 1 with an API token (or the password), moves them to the new product's WHM package on an upgrade or downgrade, and changes their password.
- Plesk module: gives each service its own Plesk customer and a subscription for its domain on the product's service plan, through the XML API with a secret key (or the password). Suspend and unsuspend switch the subscription off and on, terminate deletes the customer, an upgrade or downgrade switches the plan, and change password sets both the customer's and the FTP password.
- DirectAdmin module: creates, suspends, unsuspends and deletes DirectAdmin users on the product's user package and the server's shared IP, through the CMD_API commands, and changes packages and passwords.
- Proxmox VE module: clones the product's cloud-init template into a new VM with its CPU cores, memory and disk size, sets the login user and password through cloud-init, gives it the first free address from the server's IP pool, and starts it. Suspend shuts it down, unsuspend starts it, terminate deletes it with its disks and frees the address, an upgrade or downgrade resizes it and restarts it, and change password goes through the QEMU guest agent. Logs in with an API token, or a user and password.
- Every server module picks a username from the domain and a random password when a service is created, unless staff set them first, and checks for an account an earlier run already made before making another. Staff see the server, domain, username and password in a service's **Provisioning** box and can change them under **Change server or login**; a new password on a running account is set on the server too, and the client keeps the old one until the server has taken it.
- `App\Provisioning\ChangesPassword`: modules that implement it get a **Change password** action.
- `App\Provisioning\UsesServers`: modules that implement it are set up on servers, with their own server fields, test connection and login details. A new control panel module can extend `App\Provisioning\Modules\ServerModule`, which runs each action on the service's server and gives a new service its login, and talk to the panel through a `ServerApi` client like the built-in ones.
- `ProvisioningResult::later()`: a module still waiting on the other system (such as Proxmox copying a disk) asks to be run again after a wait instead of failing, for up to an hour.
- The WHMCS importer brings servers and server groups over: cPanel, Plesk, DirectAdmin and Proxmox servers with their host, IP addresses, port, login, access hash (as the API token), maximum accounts and on/off switch, and groups with their fill type and servers. A server already added here with the same host name is used as it is. Products on those WHMCS modules get the module, their package or service plan, and their server group; services get their server, username and dedicated IP, and active and suspended ones count as set up. With WHMCS's encryption hash (`--hash=` or the walkthrough's new **WHMCS encryption hash** box, kept encrypted in the staff member's session), server and service passwords come over too; without it, or with a wrong one, the report says they were left out.
- Clients change their control panel password from the service's portal page (at least 10 characters, with upper and lower case letters and a number, typed twice). The new password is set on the server from the queue, and the old one keeps working until it has; the page says if the change didn't go through.
- Control panel single sign-on: **Log in to the control panel** on a running service's portal page opens cPanel (WHM `create_user_session`), Plesk (an XML API session tied to the client's IP address) or DirectAdmin (a one-time login URL) already logged in. Staff have the same button on the admin service page. Modules offer it by implementing `App\Provisioning\SingleSignOn`; a login address on any host but the service's server is refused.
- Moving an account to another server: **Move to another server** on the admin service page creates the account on another active server of the same module with the same domain, username and password, and keeps the old one until staff have copied the data and press **Finish the move**, which removes it. Ticking "nothing to copy" removes the old account as soon as the new one is ready. A move whose new account fails can be cancelled back to the old server, a failed removal can be tried again, a suspended service is suspended on its new server, and a service that ends mid-move has its old account removed too. The client's portal page says the service is moving.
- Upgrades and downgrades between products of different server modules, such as cPanel to Plesk, by clients and staff: the account moves to a server of the new product's module (from its server group), keeping the domain and password with a username the new module picks, and the old account stays until staff finish the move, as above. Staff are emailed when the new account is ready to copy to, and the upgrade pages say what will happen. Changing between a Proxmox VM product and a container product moves the account the same way (modules flag such products with `App\Provisioning\SplitsAccountKinds`), and a service can't be upgraded again until its move is finished or cancelled.
- Proxmox LXC containers: a Proxmox product's new **Type** setting makes its services containers, created from an OS template (**Container template**) with the product's cores, memory and root disk (8 GB when blank) on its **Network bridge**, an address from the server's IP pool (DHCP without one) and the service's password for root. Suspend, unsuspend, terminate and upgrades work on containers as on VMs; containers take a new size without a restart. A container's password can't be changed through Proxmox, so it isn't offered.
- Reinstalling a Proxmox VM or container from its template: staff run **Reinstall** on the service page, and clients can from the portal after ticking that everything will be erased. The old one is stopped and deleted, and a new one is made with the same VM ID, address and password. Modules offer it by implementing `App\Provisioning\Reinstalls`.
- Module settings can be a dropdown (`'type' => 'select'` with `'options'`).
- Clients see how to log in to a service on a server (control panel address or IP address, username and password) on its portal page once it's set up.
- Automatic copies on server moves: **Move to another server** can have the module copy the account with its data instead of making an empty one. cPanel runs WHM's transfer tool on the new server, logging in to the old one over SSH as root; Proxmox migrates the VM or container to the new server's node inside one cluster (live for a running VM), or copies it to another cluster with remote migration using the new server's API token. The move then waits for staff to check the copy and finish it, or removes the old account at once with the box ticked; a Proxmox VM migrated inside its cluster has nothing left to remove, so its move finishes on its own. A failed copy can be run again or the move cancelled, and copies may run for up to a day. Modules offer it by implementing `App\Provisioning\CopiesAccounts`.
- Usage statistics: `php artisan usage:collect`, run hourly by the scheduler, reads each account's disk use and this month's bandwidth, with the panel's limits, from cPanel (`listaccts` and `showbw`), Plesk (subscription size, traffic and limits), DirectAdmin (user usage and limits) and Proxmox (disk and a running traffic counter, added up across restarts). They are kept per month and shown with bars on the admin and portal service pages. Modules report usage by implementing `App\Provisioning\ReportsUsage`.
- Usage-based billing: a product's **Usage billing** settings include an amount of disk and monthly bandwidth (in MB) and charge a price per GB over. After each month `billing:run` invoices each running service's overage, with a line per resource and one invoice per client, due in `BILLING_OVERAGE_DUE_DAYS` days (7 by default). Each month is billed once. Overage prices are in the billing currency, so clients in another currency aren't charged.
- Software licensing module: each service is a license key for your own software, issued when the service starts, suspended and expired with it. Software checks its key at `POST /api/licenses/check` with its domain, directory and a token; the first checks lock the license to the domain, IP address and directory (as the product's **Lock to** and **Installs per license** set), and answers are signed with the product's secret in `X-License-Signature`. Clients see the key and where it is locked on the service page and can reissue it, unless the product leaves that to staff. See [docs/licensing.md](docs/licensing.md).
- Server status monitoring: `php artisan servers:check`, every five minutes from the scheduler, checks each server's module port and any other ports set under **Status monitoring** on the server, and reads cPanel's load and each Proxmox node's CPU, memory, uptime and whether it is online. A server is down when its module port doesn't answer and in warning when another port or a node is out. Two checks in a row must agree before the status changes; then admins are emailed, the dashboard lists the server, and the outage is recorded. The servers page shows each server's status, load and 30-day uptime, and **Check status** checks one now. Modules report a server's health by implementing `App\Provisioning\ReportsServerHealth`.
- Module settings can set a shortest length for text (`'min'`).
